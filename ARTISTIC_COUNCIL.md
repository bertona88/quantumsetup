# Artistic Council

Status: implemented arrangement doctrine for Infinite Techno `1.5.0`

## Constitution

The council is a design-room metaphor, not a simulation of four people. Carl Cox,
Sven Väth, Richie Hawtin, and Derrick May did not participate in, approve, or
endorse this project. Their names identify broad critical lenses the generator uses
to interrogate its own arrangement. The implementation does not clone their music,
voices, catalogs, or personal opinions.

The council exists because the expanded instrument bank exposed the wrong question:
not “what else can play?” but “what deserves the foreground now?”

## The four lenses

| Reference | Internal lens | Hard question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Carl Cox | Floor Authority | Does the kick-and-bass record still read physically? |
| Sven Väth | Long Arc | Does this change earn a place across the long form? |
| Richie Hawtin | Radical Reduction | What can disappear until one idea feels inevitable? |
| Derrick May | Machine Soul | Where are swing, memory, identity, and emotional voltage? |

These are intentionally concise editorial principles, not artist presets.

## Decision rule

There is no section-to-chair map. At every eight-bar phrase, all four lenses receive
a deterministic score from the current recurrent state:

- Floor Authority responds to weak floor trust, low physical energy, and kick
  withdrawal. It may recognize an earned release or floor recommit, but any
  physical kick-family morph is recurrent-state-owned, phrase-interpolated, and
  followed by a 24-phrase cooldown;
- Long Arc responds to accumulated payoff debt, trusted continuity, and time since
  a climax;
- Radical Reduction responds to fatigue, contrast debt, and overfull energy;
- Machine Soul responds to novelty debt, weakened motif salience, and lineage
  memory.

Named coordinate jitter breaks close ties. A short incumbent-residency bonus keeps
the chair from flickering every phrase; an overlong-residency penalty eventually
opens the decision again. Climax forces Floor Authority and release forces Radical
Reduction, but neither event is assigned to a section or bar in advance.

The winning chair does not average four suggestions:

1. apply one bounded phrase-state change;
2. preserve, thin, or deliberately withdraw the floor according to earned state;
3. choose the smallest advanced-synth cast that can express the phrase;
4. remove optional layers that compete in the same spectrum;
5. allow a second advanced voice only when the current phenotype and recurrent
   phrase state justify dialogue;
6. allow a fill only when recurrent state grants permission;
7. keep equal-priority foregrounds resident until a causal motif event authorizes
   a handoff.

Section names are run-length readouts derived after the decision. They describe
what emerged; they do not assign a chair, energy curve, climax, fill, or transition.
They also do not select a scene, tonal identity, bass voice, or synth engine.

A motif `mutate`, `replace`, or `recall` event may authorize exactly one Matrix,
Resonator, or String handoff at its stable phrase boundary. `hold` authorizes none.
The event coordinates score the engine directly, so there is no 16-bar clock or
round-robin. Taste can rank candidates only inside that already-authorized handoff;
it cannot create one.

The output is a generic directive—`FLOOR FIRST`, `PLAY THE LONG ARC`,
`REMOVE UNTIL IT MATTERS`, or `LET THE MACHINE SING`—plus a phrase phase:
Declare, Hold, Turn, or Reveal. Artist names never appear in the listener-facing
control surface.

## Non-negotiable vetoes

- Never play all three advanced engines together.
- Never let taste feedback rewrite rhythm, harmony, arrangement, or energy.
- Never let taste feedback authorize its own synth change.
- Never add an attack to a quarter-note kick anchor.
- Never relocate more than one priority lead to solve a collision.
- Never make a dramatic fill routine.
- Never mistake more instruments for more musical information.
- Never treat a derived section label as an arrangement schedule.
- Never let a 192-bar observation/cache boundary change musical identity.

The council supplies deterministic editorial pressure. It does not establish
subjective musical quality; that still requires long listening.
